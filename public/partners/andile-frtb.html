The problem

REGULATORY REPORTING IS BROKEN

FRTB and Basel IV require banks to produce detailed commentary alongside raw capital numbers. Today, that commentary is written manually by senior analysts who are too expensive to use as report writers.

Today — manual process
  • ⏱Senior risk analyst spends 2–4 hours per reporting section writing narrative commentary from raw Calypso or FinMechanics exports
  • 🔁Commentary reviewed, revised, and re-reviewed through multiple sign-off cycles — same analyst re-drafting the same structure every cycle
  • ⚠️Inconsistent language and framing across reporting periods creates audit exposure — regulators flag style changes as material
  • 💸Cost: 1 FTE analyst day per reporting pack, every reporting cycle. At £600/day contractor rate, this is £150K+ per year in report writing alone
  • 🚨FRTB IMA attestation deadlines routinely missed when markets move significantly and commentary must be re-written from scratch
With FRTB Commentary Engine
  • ⚡Raw risk export (CSV / JSON / XML) ingested directly — AI reads the numbers, understands the context, writes the commentary
  • 📋Consistent regulatory language across every cycle — the AI uses a house style template trained on your past approved reports
  • ✅Exceptions are flagged, explained, and contextualised automatically — analyst reviews and approves, doesn't author
  • 🌍Jurisdiction-aware: SARB (South Africa), PRA (UK), APRA (Australia), RBI (India) — correct local regulatory tone and citation format per output
  • 🔒Deployed in the bank's own Cloudflare account — data never leaves their jurisdiction. POPIA, GDPR, APRA compliant by architecture

5.2 Internal Models Approach — Back-Testing Results (Q4 2024)

During Q4 2024, the Rates and Derivatives desk (RD-001, RD-003) recorded four VaR back-testing exceptions, placing the desk in the Red Zone under the Basel IV IMA framework (BCBS 457, §99.5). This triggers a mandatory multiplier add-on of +1.0 to the IMA capital multiplier, effective from the next reporting date.

The exceptions on 14 October and 03 December 2024 are attributable to elevated rates volatility following central bank policy divergence between the US Federal Reserve and ECB — a market dislocation not anticipated within the historical simulation window.

Management has reviewed these exceptions in accordance with §99.7 of the BCBS standard. The desk has submitted a formal attestation confirming that the exceptions do not reflect model deficiencies, and a remediation plan has been initiated to extend the historical lookback period from 250 to 500 trading days prior to Q1 2025 reporting.

Architecture

HOW THE ENGINE WORKS

A five-step pipeline from raw risk export to approved commentary — running entirely within the bank's own infrastructure.

01
📥
Ingest
CSV / JSON / XML exports from Calypso, FinMechanics, FIS, or any risk system. No bespoke API required.
02
⚡
Classify
Llama 3.1 at the edge classifies exception type, severity, and regulatory zone. Sub-50ms, no external API call.
03
🧠
Contextualise
Vectorize RAG retrieves relevant prior commentary, house-style templates, and regulatory citations from the bank's own document store.
04
✍️
Draft
Claude 4.6 writes the commentary section — jurisdiction-aware, regulation-cited, consistent with the bank's prior approved language.
05
✅
Review
Analyst reviews a draft, not a blank page. One-click approve or redline. Approved commentary updates the RAG store for future cycles.
Technical pipeline — edge-deployed, bank's own infrastructure
1
Risk export lands in R2 object store
Calypso / FinMechanics / FIS pushes the export to the bank's Cloudflare R2 bucket via a scheduled job or manual upload. Stays within the bank's account — no external transfer.
Cloudflare R2 · wrangler r2 object put
2
Worker triggers on upload, parses and classifies
A Cloudflare Worker fires on the R2 upload event. Parses the export format, runs Llama 3.1 8B via Workers AI to classify each exception by type (market dislocation, model breach, data quality) and severity zone (Green/Amber/Red).
Workers AI · @cf/meta/llama-3.1-8b-instruct
3
RAG retrieval from document store
Vectorize queries the bank's embedded document store — prior approved commentaries, regulatory guidance (BCBS 457, PRA SS11/13, APRA APS 116), and house-style templates. Returns the 5 most relevant prior passages as context.
Cloudflare Vectorize · text-embedding-3-small
4
Claude 4.6 drafts the commentary section
A structured prompt containing the parsed exception data, RAG context, regulatory citations, jurisdiction flag, and house-style instructions is sent to Claude Sonnet 4.6 via the 2nth.ai AI Gateway. Commentary is returned in the bank's approved format.
claude-sonnet-4-6 via AI Gateway · max 2048 tokens
5
Draft stored in D1, surfaced in review UI
The drafted commentary is stored in D1 (SQLite at edge) with full version history. The analyst's review portal — a Cloudflare Pages app — shows the draft alongside the raw data, diff view from the prior cycle, and one-click approve/redline controls.
D1 SQLite · Cloudflare Pages · version-controlled
